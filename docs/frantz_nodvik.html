    How it is implemented

    Model

    The Frantz–Nodvik model describes a pulse crossing a gain medium whose inversion is only changed by the pulse itself: no pumping, no spontaneous decay and no amplified spontaneous emission during the pulse, homogeneous gain, a plane wave with no dispersion or nonlinearity. In a frame moving with the pulse the rate equations reduce to

    ∂I∂z=g⁢I, ∂g∂t=−g⁢IJsat, Jsat=hνσa+σe

    where g=σeN2−σaN1 is the gain coefficient. Writing the gain with both cross sections makes the same equations hold for three-level and quasi-three-level media such as Er and Yb fibre: the gain then relaxes towards transparency rather than towards zero inversion, and the saturation fluence carries σa+σe instead of σe alone. For a four-level medium set σa=0.

    Closed-form solution

    Integrating the second equation gives g(z,t)=g0(z)⁢exp[−J(z,t)/Jsat], with J(z,t) the fluence that has passed the point z up to time t. Substituting into the first equation and using y=exp(J/Jsat) turns it into dy/dz=g0(z)(y−1), which integrates to the Frantz–Nodvik result for the cumulative fluence:

    Jout(t)=Jsat⁢ln{1+G0[exp(Jin(t)Jsat)−1]}, G0=exp(∫0Lg0dz)

    Only the integrated small-signal gain enters, so the total output is exact even when the inversion varies along the fibre. Differentiating with respect to time gives the output power and the instantaneous gain seen by each slice of the pulse:

    Iout(t)=Iin(t)⁢G(t), G(t)=G0eu1+G0(eu−1)=11−e−u(1−1/G0), u(t)=Jin(t)/Jsat

    The gain starts at G0 for the leading edge and falls towards 1 as the pulse extracts the inversion, which is why the front of the pulse is amplified more than the back: the output steepens, its peak moves forward and, for a Gaussian, it narrows. How much depends on two numbers only: G0Ein/Esat decides whether saturation starts at all (below about 0.3 the pulse comes out with the input shape, just larger), and Ein/Esat decides how low the trailing edge's gain falls. Both appear in the amplifier panel, and the sweep charts show the whole curve.

    Backwards: the input that produces a wanted output

    The fluence relation is monotonic, so it inverts in closed form. Given the wanted output you get the required input directly, with no iteration:

    Jin(t)=Jsat⁢ln{1+exp(Jout(t)/Jsat)−1G0}, Iin(t)=Iout(t)1+(G0−1)e−v, v(t)=Jout(t)/Jsat

    This is the pre-compensation used in shaped-seed fibre MOPAs [5, 6]: a square output needs a seed that ramps up towards its trailing edge to make up for the gain the front of the pulse has already consumed. The required input always exists; whether it is practical is another matter, and the warnings flag outputs that exceed the stored energy.

    Energies, not fluences

    For a single transverse mode the fluence is replaced by energy through an effective area. In the “Esat & gain” mode this is the plain beam area, Esat=Jsatπd2/4 for a 1/e² diameter d. The extractable stored energy and the extraction efficiency follow from the small-signal gain:

    Estored=Esat⁢lnG0, η=Eout−EinEstored

    From a fibre datasheet to the model

    The “Fibre datasheet” mode follows the same derivation the Python package uses in fiber.py. The absorption figure fixes the dopant concentration, the geometry fixes the mode and its overlap with the doped core, and the inversion fixes the gain:

    N=αref4.343⁢Γref⁢σa(λref), wa=0.65+1.619V−3/2+2.879V−6, Γ=1−e−2a2/w2
    Esat=hν⁢AcoreΓ(σa+σe), G0=exp{ΓNL[(σa+σe)n2−σa]}, Estored=hν⁢NAcoreL(n2−ntr)

    Here a is the core radius, V = 2πa·NA/λ, w the LP01 mode-field radius from Marcuse's fit, Γ the overlap of the mode with the doped core, and ntr=σa/(σa+σe) the transparency inversion. A core-absorption figure uses Γ at λref; a cladding figure (the pump absorption of a double-clad fibre) uses Γ = Acore/Aclad. When the datasheet gives the mode-field diameter instead of the NA, the fit is inverted to get the V-number and the overlap at other wavelengths follows from it. The saturation energy with Acore/Γ is the Giles-model form, about half the π w² area for a small core: it reproduces the onset of saturation set by the peak intensity of the mode. The cross sections come from the tables shipped with this repository (representative values, not vendor data), interpolated at the signal, reference and pump wavelengths.

    Pump estimate

    With “gain from pump estimate” the pre-pulse inversion is not entered but solved from an energy balance between pulses. The fibre is treated as one lumped reservoir of Ntot=NAcoreL ions. Each pulse removes one ion from the upper state per photon it gains; between pulses the pump refills it and the lifetime drains it:

    dn2dt=Pabs(n2)hνpNtot−n2τ, Pabs=Pp[1−exp(−ΓpNL[σap(1−n2)−σepn2])]

    The periodic steady state (pulse, recovery over one period, same inversion again) is found by bisection on the pre-pulse inversion, integrating the recovery with an adaptive Runge–Kutta step. Two caps apply: the pump cannot invert the medium beyond σap/(σap+σep) (0.5 for Yb pumped at 976 nm, close to 1 for Er at 976 nm), and the “ASE gain cap” clamps the inversion so the single-pass gain never exceeds it, standing in for the ASE that would otherwise drain a long, highly doped fibre at a low repetition rate. This is a design estimate: it has no position dependence along the fibre, no ASE spectrum, no excited-state absorption, upconversion or ion pairing, and the pump is continuous. For those, use the full solver in the Python package.

    Numerics

    • Each shape is defined with unit width and calibrated numerically once (FWHM, peak position and the support where it exceeds 10⁻⁶ of the peak), then scaled to the requested FWHM. The grid has 4096 samples over that support with 4 % padding, so the Q-switch tail and the custom table are resolved on the same footing as the analytic shapes. Shapes follow the definitions in the Python package: Gaussian exp[−4 ln2 (t/T)²], super-Gaussian exp[−ln2 (2|t|/T)2m], sech²(1.7627 t/T), a square with logistic edges whose 10–90 % time is set as a fraction of the FWHM, and the asymmetric Q-switch shape 1/[exp(−t/τr) + exp(t/τf)]² with the fall/rise ratio as the free parameter.
    • The cumulative energy is accumulated sample by sample and the closed form is evaluated at the midpoint of every sample. No differential equation is integrated for the pulse itself, so the result has no step-size error beyond the sampling of the pulse. Total energies use the exact fluence relation.
    • The forms log1p(G₀·expm1(u)) and 1/(1 − e−u(1 − 1/G₀)) keep full precision for weak saturation (u ≪ 1) and for gains of 60 dB; for u above 30 the asymptotic expansion u + ln G₀ + log1p[(1/G₀ − 1)e−u] is used so nothing overflows. The same equations hold for G₀ below unity, where the medium is a saturable absorber.
    • The z-slices evaluate the same closed form with G₀ replaced by G₀z/L, which assumes the small-signal gain per unit length is uniform. The energy sweep repeats the forward (or inverse) calculation for 90 energies spaced logarithmically from 10⁻³ to 30 Esat, extended to include the current operating point; in pump-estimate mode the inversion is held at the solved value during the sweep.
    • FWHM is measured by linear interpolation of the half-maximum crossings on each side of the global maximum; rise and fall are the 10–90 % crossings on either side of the peak.

    What it does not include

    • Pumping and decay during the pulse. The model needs the pulse to be much shorter than the upper-state lifetime (about 10 ms for Er, 1 ms for Yb in silica, 230 µs for Nd:YAG, 3.2 µs for Ti:sapphire); a warning appears when the pulse is longer than a tenth of it.
    • Coherent effects, finite gain bandwidth and dispersion, which matter below a picosecond or so.
    • Fibre nonlinearities (self-phase modulation, Raman, Brillouin), amplified spontaneous emission during the pulse, excited-state absorption, ion pairing and the radial inversion profile. The Python package in this repository (pulse.fn_deplete and pulse.propagate_pulse) generalises the Frantz–Nodvik pass to rings, modes and paired ions and adds those effects.
